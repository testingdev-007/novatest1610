Prompting with Copilot: ask better, get better

Copilot is a teammate, not an answer machine. The clearer your question, the more useful its help. Here are the habits that make the difference.

A simple recipe for a good prompt

You don't need magic words. Just give Copilot enough to work with:

1. Context2. What I see3. What I expected4. What I want from you
  • Context: which file or section you're looking at.
  • What I see: describe the actual behaviour or look.
  • What I expected: describe what should happen instead.
  • What I want: an explanation, some options, a review, or a small change.

Weak vs strong prompts

Weak
"Fix this."

Copilot has to guess what's wrong, and may change the wrong thing.

Strong
"In this file, the button on the form does nothing when I click it. I expected a message to appear. Can you explain what could cause that before suggesting a fix?"
Weak
"Make it look better."

"Better" means different things to different people.

Strong
"Give me three options to make the text on this card easier to read for someone with poor eyesight. Explain the trade-offs of each."

Tips for fixing bugs

Describe, don't diagnose

Say what you saw and what you expected. Let Copilot help work out the cause.

Explain first, fix second

Ask "why is this happening?" before "how do I fix it?" You'll learn more and spot wrong answers faster.

Ask about one thing

Point to a specific section or line numbers instead of the whole file.

Test the whole picture

A fix for one symptom can hide a deeper cause. Check that related things still agree with each other.

Try different inputs

Try empty, zero, negative, very big and unusual values. Ask Copilot what edge cases to think about.

Use clues

Error messages and warnings are hints. Paste them into your prompt exactly as shown.

Tips for design changes

Name the person

Say who the design is for, such as a first-time user, someone on a phone, or someone using only a keyboard.

Ask for options

Design rarely has one right answer. Ask for two or three, then choose and say why.

Think about everyone

Ask about readability, colour contrast, text size, focus outlines and screen readers.

Check small screens

Ask how the layout should behave on a phone, then actually test it.

Be specific about style

Mention the feel you want (calm, bold, friendly) and any colours or branding to keep.

Ask for the reasoning

"Why is this better?" helps you decide and helps you explain your choice later.

Golden rules

  • Never keep a change you can't explain. Ask Copilot to walk you through it.
  • Copilot can be wrong. Always test what it suggests.
  • Follow up. If an answer isn't right, say what's still wrong and try again.
  • Small steps win. One request, one change, one test.
  • Ask it to review your work and look for things you might have missed.
Handy follow-up prompts
  • "Can you explain that line by line?"
  • "What else could cause this?"
  • "What could go wrong with this change?"
  • "What should I test to be sure it works?"
  • "Is there a simpler way to do this?"

Try it: take your next prompt and add what you saw, what you expected and what you want back. Notice how the answer changes.
